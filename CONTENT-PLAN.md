# Content plan — openfullpage-site replacement

**Premise:** Twenty tools. Free forever. Safe to use. In that order.

The old draft led with proof ("we do all this and send nothing"). That argues a case.
This one makes an offer: here is a well-stocked toolbox, it costs nothing, and it is
safe to use. Safety is a property of the thing, not the pitch.

Two numbers carry the page: **20** tools and **$0** forever.

## Feature inventory (20 capabilities)

### Capture (6)
1. Full page of any length, one image
2. Visible area only
3. One selected element
4. Sticky and fixed elements held still
5. Waits for lazy-loaded images
6. Cross-origin iframe contents (optional, off by default)

### Mark up (8)
7. Arrows   8. Lines   9. Boxes   10. Ellipses
11. Highlighter   12. Text   13. Numbered steps   14. Crop

### Correct (2)
15. Undo   16. Redo

### Protect (1)
17. Blur out anything private. Resamples the pixels and flattens on export, so the
    original is not recoverable from the saved file.

### Get it out (3)
18. Save as PNG   19. Save as JPEG   20. Copy to clipboard

## Section arc

1. **Hero.** The offer, plus a what-you-get card: 20 tools, $0 forever, and four
   reassurances (no account, no ads, open source, captures stay local).
2. **Everything it does.** All twenty as an uneven coloured mosaic. Uneven on purpose:
   an even grid reads as a taxonomy, an uneven one reads as abundance.
3. **Three steps.** Capture, mark it up, save or copy, each with a real screenshot.
4. **Free, and safe to use.** Five plain-language claims with the mechanism named, and
   the empty network panel as supporting evidence rather than the centrepiece.
5. **Genuinely free. No catch.** The commercial question answered head on.
6. **Questions.** Six, plainly answered.

## Content rules
- Lead with what they get. Free second. Safe third. Never reverse this.
- Safety in warm, plain language. Name the mechanism once, then move on.
- Real product screenshots only. The extension UI already displays
  `1265 x 6022 pixels` and "The image never leaves your computer" on screen.
- No testimonials, user counts or star ratings. No evidence for them, and the
  category's fake versions are why nobody believes privacy claims.
- No third-party requests from the site itself, fonts included. The site should
  pass the test the product passes.

## Carried over from the previous site
The five enforced rules, the per-permission table and the five-minute
self-verification steps now live on `enforced.html`, which is the previous landing
page kept intact and re-canonicalised. It is linked from the masthead, from the end
of the safety section, and from the footer. It is a Read surface: structure over
persuasion, and it deliberately keeps the older, quieter visual treatment.
